START HERE · THE VISION IN FIVE MINUTES

From asking business questions to running an intelligent business

We are building a platform that understands a company's business, continuously notices what matters, investigates why things happen, and helps people make better decisions—with AI agents they can create and improve themselves.

One-sentence pitch: Connect your business data, ask questions in plain language, create your own AI business agents, and let the platform proactively monitor, investigate, recommend, and learn—while people remain in control of important decisions.
UnderstandConnect data, goals and context
Act intelligentlyCreate governed AI agents
Keep improvingMeasure and learn over time

The problem we're solving

Business data is scattered

Sales, finance, customers, operations and documents are disconnected. People spend time finding answers instead of acting.

Dashboards wait for people

Traditional dashboards show numbers, but rarely investigate changes, connect departments, or explain what to do next.

Insights don't become learning

Decisions, reasons and outcomes are often forgotten. The same problems are investigated repeatedly.

What people will be able to do

Talk to their business

Ask “Why did profit fall?” and get understandable answers, charts, calculations and evidence.

Get proactive discoveries

Know what changed, why it may have changed, what opportunities exist and which issues deserve attention.

Create their own agents

Describe a responsibility in plain language; the system helps design, connect, test and launch an agent.

Build AI teams

Agents can coordinate across sales, finance, customers and operations, and delegate limited tasks to specialists.

Keep people in control

Contracts specify exactly what agents may access, how much they can spend and which actions need approval.

Improve continuously

Agents collect feedback, compare new approaches in tests, learn approved lessons and improve safely over time.

How the product grows beyond today's AI chat

Today · Ask & understand

Natural-language questions, generated database queries, conversational follow-ups, charts and explanations.

Next · Monitor & investigate

What Changed Engine, evidence-backed investigations, Opportunity Hunter, Decision Memory and Outcome Tracker.

Vision · Organize & improve

Customer-created agents, temporary specialists, shared learning, workforce recommendations and tested self-improvement.

Product maturity note: “Today” reflects the currently described capabilities. “Next” and “Vision” are planned concepts, not claims that these features are already available.

See it through one simple story

Meet a retail business owner

They connect sales, inventory and customer data. On Monday, the system notices that revenue fell while deliveries slowed. It investigates the relevant products and regions, presents evidence, and suggests actions. The owner creates a Sales Guardian agent to watch this daily. When a complex supply problem appears, the agent uses a temporary specialist. The owner approves any consequential action. The platform tracks whether the decision helped and tests ways to improve future alerts.

What changed? Instead of only answering one question, the platform helps the business notice, investigate, decide, follow through and learn.

Why this is different from a chatbot, dashboard or generic agent builder

Not just a chatbot

It can monitor goals and business events even when nobody is asking a question.

Not just a dashboard

It investigates important changes, explains evidence, and connects findings to decisions and outcomes.

Not just an agent builder

Agents are grounded in business context, governed by contracts, evaluated on usefulness, and organized around company goals.

Our ultimate goal: A business can describe what it wants to achieve. The platform helps assemble the right AI workforce, gives each agent a clear mission, monitors results, recommends improvements, and preserves organizational learning. Humans retain authority over business-critical decisions.

ONE COMPLETE EXAMPLE

From an idea to a self-improving agent

A straightforward story showing how all the major modules fit together.

Step 1 · Describe the job⌄
The sales manager says: “Check sales every morning, investigate unusual drops, and send me a summary.”
Step 2 · AI designs the agent⌄
Agent Studio proposes its goal, schedule, sales metrics, investigation skills, report format and estimated cost.
Step 3 · Set the rules⌄
Agent Contract allows read-only sales access and internal reporting; price changes and customer messages are forbidden without approval.
Step 4 · Test safely⌄
The agent replays historical sales drops. The team checks whether it found the right patterns and avoided misleading claims.
Step 5 · Activate and monitor⌄
Every morning, the shared runtime runs the agent. Results, sources, costs and any errors appear in the Operations Center.
Step 6 · Investigate with specialists⌄
For a complex decline, it may delegate bounded work to approved pricing or customer specialists; each has limited access and lifetime.
Step 7 · Learn from feedback⌄
Users flag noisy Monday alerts. The agent proposes a weekday-adjusted baseline. The Evaluation Lab compares it against held-out historical cases.
Step 8 · Approve a better version⌄
If tests pass, an authorized owner approves the new version. The platform tracks results and can roll back if performance worsens.

CHAPTER 01

Vision & product strategy

Why the platform exists

North star⌄
Build an always-on business reasoning and decision platform: Observe → Reason → Remember → Decide → Act with human approval → Measure → Learn. The current foundation is natural-language database questions, generated queries, contextual follow-ups, charts, tables, and explanatory insights.
Three product pillars⌄
1. Conversational Intelligence: ask and explore business data. 2. Business Reasoning Engine: detect change, investigate, discover opportunities, and track decisions. 3. Business Agent Studio: let customers create, supervise, coordinate, and improve agents.
Design principle⌄
Organize the product around measurable business goals rather than the number of agents created. Business Goal → Workforce Design → Agent Contracts → Collaborative Execution → Decisions → Outcomes → Learning.
Example⌄
A retailer says: “Reduce stockouts and protect gross margin.” The platform proposes an Inventory Agent, Margin Guardian, and shared escalation rules, then measures their actual usefulness.

CHAPTER 04

Agent Contracts & governance

Living agreement, not just a system prompt

Three separate objects⌄
Agent Identity = owner, role, and capabilities. Agent Contract = mission, boundaries, evidence, and obligations. Performance Record = what actually happened, under which version.
Mission dimensions⌄
1 Responsibility: supported outcome. 2 Scope: products, customers, regions, metrics. 3 Success criteria: useful performance. 4 Priority rules: how objectives conflict.
Authority dimensions⌄
5 Permissions: data/tools/actions. 6 Autonomy: per-action authority. 7 Delegation: which tasks may be assigned. 8 Prohibitions: explicit hard restrictions.
Operations dimensions⌄
9 Triggers: schedules and events. 10 Budget: spend, tokens, time, rate, depth. 11 Evidence standards: reproducible proof requirements. 12 Escalation: when humans intervene.
Accountability dimensions⌄
13 Human owner. 14 Independent evaluation. 15 Expiration/review lifecycle. 16 Change control and version approvals.
Authority ladder⌄
L0 Observer: read/report. L1 Investigator: bounded analysis. L2 Advisor: recommendations. L3 Operator with approval: prepare actions. L4 Bounded operator: explicitly allowlisted low-risk actions. Enforce per action, not globally.
Hierarchical contract model⌄
Business Outcome → Agent Responsibility → Executable Tasks. Example: Reduce churn → monitor at-risk accounts → compute risk changes, investigate drivers, draft recommendations.
Contract negotiation⌄
If user says “guarantee zero churn,” AI proposes a measurable support objective and explains limits, data gaps, tradeoffs, and estimated costs.
Contract health⌄
Detect stale data, revoked credentials, missing tools, impossible SLAs, budget exhaustion, or conflicting policies. Pause affected functions and alert owners.
Conflict graph⌄
Sales Agent proposes discount; Finance Guardian finds margin-policy conflict; system escalates to designated decision owner instead of overriding policy.
Evidence contract⌄
Metric claim needs definition and reproducible calculation; causal claim needs credible causal evidence or explicit uncertainty; forecast needs backtesting and interval; action recommendation needs risk and cost analysis.
Economic contract⌄
Track spend, time, false-alert burden, cost per useful finding, human review effort, and verified—not merely projected—business impact.
Memory boundaries⌄
Separate working memory, operational history, approved decision memory, and restricted sensitive data. Retention and access are governed.
Handover & succession⌄
Replacing an agent transfers approved work and knowledge, but never silently transfers expired permissions or secrets.
Contract lifecycle⌄
Draft → Negotiate → Simulate → Approve → Activate → Supervise → Review → Renew/Amend/Suspend/Retire.
Contract simulation⌄
Replay historical data as available at the time to measure detection delay, false alarms, evidence quality, budget, and forbidden action attempts.
Enforcement engine⌄
LLM proposes tool calls; deterministic backend enforces tenant isolation, identities, data scopes, read/write policy, approval, quotas, logging, and emergency revocation. Never rely only on prompt instructions.
Illustrative contract⌄
Revenue Protection Agent: daily sales monitoring, read-only authorized data, max $40/month, report with SQL evidence, no pricing updates, escalate critical declines, 30-day review. Numbers are examples, not defaults.
PRODUCT DECISION · SHARING, NOT SELLING

Agent Sharing & Reuse — no selling

People can share useful agents and skills so teams do not recreate the same work. This is a collaboration and reuse capability, not a community sharing library.

Private

Keep an agent or template visible only to its creator.

Team

Share with a named team, with role-based access and optional review before installation.

Organization

Make an approved template discoverable across the company.

Optional community

Share a reusable, sanitized template with others for free, if organization policy allows it.

Clone & customize

Recipients install an independent copy and adjust the goal, data mappings, instructions and schedule.

Update responsibly

Template owners publish version notes; recipients choose whether to adopt changes after checks.

What exactly gets shared?

Included

Template description, intended goal, required skills, workflow, abstract business data concepts, configurable instructions, contract example, test cases and version information.

Never included

API keys, credentials, private conversations, customer data, tenant-specific records, restricted memories or the original agent's permissions.

Required when installing

Map to the recipient's approved data sources, validate tool access, assign a human owner, confirm budgets and permissions, run tests, and explicitly activate.

Product boundary: No agent selling, paid listings, seller accounts, checkout, commissions, or template subscriptions. The library exists to make knowledge and useful agents easier to share and reuse safely.
CHAPTER 05

Five frontier capabilities

Self-creation, agent sharing, learning, temporary teams, workforce architect

1. Agents create agents⌄
Distinguish bounded temporary specialists, permanent agents requiring approval, and proposed agents for recurring work. Parent detects capability gap → searches existing skills/agents → drafts specialist contract → checks budget/authority → delegates → validates output → terminates or proposes permanent role.
Delegation safeguards⌄
No recursive unlimited spawning. Enforce maximum depth, concurrency, cost, lifetime, tool access, and narrower permissions than parent; all children traceable to a human-approved mission.
2. Share and reuse agents⌄
Team library → organization library → optional free community sharing. Shared templates contain agent definitions, required business concepts, skill dependencies, test cases, compatibility notes, ownership, access rules and version history. They never include customer records, credentials, secrets or private memory. Recipients make their own governed copy and must independently authorize data access and actions.
Adaptive installation⌄
Map a retail inventory template’s concepts (product, stock, supplier, orders) onto each customer's approved schema and metrics; validate before activation.
3. Agents learn from agents⌄
Publish governed findings/methods with source lineage, confidence, time validity, permissions, and review status into a Business Learning Graph. Peers consume only authorized, applicable knowledge.
Shared learning categories⌄
Verified facts; tested analytical methods; decision/outcome lessons; operational feedback such as false-positive patterns. Avoid uncontrolled copying of private memory.
4. Temporary agents⌄
Mission types: fixed-duration, event-ended, task-ended, manually closed, renewable. Example: 30-day product launch war room with Sales, Marketing, Customer, and Operations specialists.
Mission closure⌄
At end: produce final report, hand off open tasks, save approved lessons, revoke credentials, terminate triggers, and archive audit trail.
5. AI Workforce Architect⌄
Analyze business goals, available sources, team functions, and coverage gaps; recommend a minimal effective agent organization with owners, dependencies, budgets, and approval needs.
Workforce coverage map⌄
Show which goals are monitored, which have no responsible agent, where responsibilities overlap, and which dependencies need collaboration.
Agent Discovery Engine⌄
Suggest automations when the platform notices repeated approved investigations or recurring manual analyses.
Workforce Optimization⌄
Recommend merging redundant agents, retiring unused agents, adjusting run frequency, or adding shared skills based on cost and verified value.
Agent Organization Replay⌄
Timeline of which agent detected a change, delegated work, generated evidence, requested approvals, and measured outcomes.
Individual → collaborative → organizational intelligence⌄
Level 1 agent understands its mission. Level 2 agents coordinate and challenge one another. Level 3 the platform recommends the structure of the whole workforce.
CHAPTER 06

Self-creation & self-improvement engine

A controlled learning loop with independent tests

Core loop⌄
Execute → Observe results → Evaluate independently → Diagnose weakness → Propose candidate → Simulate/backtest → Approve → Deploy cautiously → Monitor → Roll back if needed.
Eight improvement surfaces⌄
Instructions, workflow ordering, tool choice, data retrieval, memory freshness, alert rules, hypothesis strategy, and model/cost routing.
No model retraining required⌄
Early improvements modify prompts, workflow graphs, retrieval, thresholds, skills, and model routing. Fine-tuning is optional later and should be independently justified.
Example: seasonal false alerts⌄
Revenue Agent sees Monday false positives; proposes weekday-adjusted baselines; tests on held-out weeks; checks precision AND recall; deploys only if guardrails pass.
Example: churn agent⌄
Candidate adds usage trend, support frequency, and seasonality. Evaluate on time-separated historical cases, avoid leakage, check subgroup performance and calibration.
Independent evaluation⌄
Measure correctness, SQL validity, evidence support, missed critical events, false positives, cost, latency, review burden, and downstream outcome quality. Agent cannot edit its own gold tests or scoring criteria.
Experiment design⌄
Freeze baseline and evaluation set; create candidate version; use replay/shadow traffic; compare quality, cost, latency, safety, and confidence intervals where appropriate.
Deployment policy⌄
Human approval for high-risk or authority-changing modifications. Only preapproved low-risk configuration tweaks may auto-promote after independent tests; all changes versioned and reversible.
Shadow mode⌄
Observe real events and prepare recommendations without external side effects. Compare against actual decisions before activation.
Version comparison⌄
Compare v1/v2 on held-out scenarios, false alerts, cost per run, evidence violations, and safety regressions; never optimize a single metric in isolation.
Self-creation workflow⌄
Detect capability gap → reuse skill/agent first → propose narrow temporary specialist → enforce inherited-or-narrower authority and budgets → execute → evaluate → retire.
Learning from rejection⌄
Capture reviewed feedback like “market share currently matters more than short-term margin,” attach owner and expiry, and treat it as a contextual preference, not a permanent fact.
Improvement maturity⌄
Stage 1 feedback capture; Stage 2 automated candidate experiments; Stage 3 bounded low-risk promotion; Stage 4 shared verified methods; Stage 5 workforce restructuring recommendations.
Rollback & kill switch⌄
Keep immutable previous versions, run-level lineage, emergency stop, approval revocation, incident handling, and monitoring for degradation after deployment.
CHAPTER 07

Technical architecture & data model

Implementation-ready conceptual blueprint

Shared runtime⌄
Do not create one server per agent. Store definitions separately from executions; orchestrate shared LLM/database tools. Interactive tasks can reuse current backend; schedule or heavy jobs can move to queue workers.
Major services⌄
Agent Architect, Registry, Factory, Runtime, Trigger Engine, Skill Engine, Tool Gateway, Contract Engine, Memory Service, Evaluation Engine, Improvement Designer, Experiment Runner, Deployment Controller, Run Ledger, Operations Center.
Core tables⌄
agents, agent_versions, agent_contracts, agent_contract_versions, agent_tools, agent_skills, agent_triggers, agent_runs, agent_tool_calls, evidence_records, approvals, budgets, evaluation_cases, evaluation_runs, improvement_proposals, agent_delegations, agent_memories, agent_templates, agent_outcomes.
Runtime execution⌄
Trigger → authenticate tenant and agent → load pinned approved contract → plan task → authorize each tool call → execute read/write under limits → verify evidence → record run → route result or approval.
Contract enforcement⌄
Enforce in backend/tool gateway and database, not in LLM text. Use least privilege, row-level/tenant scoping, action allowlists, secrets vault, rate limits, quotas, and immutable audit records.
Multi-agent orchestration⌄
Task graph with parent-child IDs, depth limits, timeouts, cancellation, retries, idempotency, result provenance, and independent approval for new persistent identities.
Memory architecture⌄
Scoped retrieval over business knowledge and run history; classify content by sensitivity, ownership, freshness, and retention. Shared learning must preserve lineage and access controls.
Evaluation infrastructure⌄
Offline golden cases, temporal replay, synthetic edge cases, red-team/policy tests, shadow runs, human adjudication, and outcome measurement.
Observability⌄
Trace IDs, contract version, tool calls, token/API cost, latency, source freshness, approval state, incident records, and performance trends.
AWS deployment at early scale⌄
For ~100 initial users and external LLM APIs, keep the existing backend and agent modules shared. Add persistent database storage and an SQS-backed worker for scheduled/long tasks when needed; scale by measured load.
Reliability details⌄
Queue retries and dead-letter handling, idempotent actions, bounded execution time, cancellation, partial-result handling, circuit breakers, source outage response, and spend caps.
Security & governance⌄
Tenant isolation, least-privilege data access, auditability, human-approved consequential actions, emergency revocation, safe template installation, data minimization, and explicit retention policies.
